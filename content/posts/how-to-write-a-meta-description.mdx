A meta description doesn't move your ranking position — but it's often
the deciding factor in whether someone clicks your result over the one
above or below it. That makes it one of the highest-leverage 160
characters you'll write.

## What a meta description actually does

It's the snippet shown under your title in search results (Google
sometimes rewrites it, but a well-written one is usually kept as-is). Its
only job is to earn the click by promising a specific, relevant answer.

## The formula

A meta description that works usually has three parts:

1. **The specific thing the reader gets** — not "learn about X" but the
   actual outcome or answer.
2. **A concrete detail** — a number, a format, a scope — that a vague
   competitor's description doesn't have.
3. **The keyword phrase**, worked in naturally, since Google bolds
   matching terms in results.

Keep it to roughly 150–160 characters; longer gets truncated with an
ellipsis, which undercuts the promise you're making.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing it last, as an afterthought.** If it's hard to summarize a
  post in one sentence, that's often a sign the post itself needs a
  tighter focus.
- **Keyword-stuffing it.** One natural mention beats three awkward ones —
  it's written for a human deciding whether to click, not a crawler.
- **Letting it auto-generate from the first paragraph.** That's better
  than nothing, but it's rarely the strongest pitch for the post.

## Try it

Take your last three published posts, write a one-sentence meta
description for each using the formula above, and compare it to what's
live now. If you can't name the specific, concrete thing the reader gets,
rewrite the description — or reconsider what the post is actually about.
