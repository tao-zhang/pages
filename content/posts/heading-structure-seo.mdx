Headings aren't just bigger text for emphasis — they're a structural
outline that both readers and search engines use to understand a page.
Get the hierarchy wrong and you make the content harder to parse for both.

## What each level is for

- **H1**: The page title, used exactly once. It tells search engines and
  readers what the entire page is about. Most blog platforms generate this
  automatically from the post title, so you rarely write it by hand.
- **H2**: Major sections. Think of these as the chapters of the post — each
  one covers a distinct sub-topic under the overall title.
- **H3**: Subsections within an H2, for when a major section needs its own
  internal breakdown. Not every H2 needs H3s underneath it.

## The rule that matters most: don't skip levels

Jumping from an H2 straight to an H4 (skipping H3) breaks the logical
outline — screen readers and search engines both rely on that hierarchy to
understand nesting, and skipping levels makes the structure ambiguous.
If you're tempted to skip a level purely to get a certain font size,
that's a signal to fix it with CSS, not with the wrong heading tag.

## Why this matters for SEO specifically

- **Featured snippets and "People Also Ask" results** are frequently pulled
  directly from a well-labeled H2 or H3 and the text immediately under it
  — a clean hierarchy makes your content easier to extract.
- **Search engines use headings to understand page structure** at a
  glance, which affects both what a page can rank for and how accurately
  a search snippet represents it.
- **Skimmers convert better than scrollers.** Most readers scan headings
  before deciding whether to read the paragraph — see our [on-page SEO
  checklist](/blog/on-page-seo-checklist) for how this fits into the
  broader structure pass every post gets.

## A quick self-audit

Read just the headings of a post, top to bottom, ignoring all the body
text. If that outline alone makes sense as a summary of the post, the
hierarchy is doing its job. If it reads like a random list of section
titles with no logical order, the structure needs another pass before
publish — and it's worth checking [meta description](/blog/how-to-write-a-meta-description)
and [internal links](/blog/internal-linking-for-seo) at the same time,
since all three tend to get fixed in the same editing pass.

## Common mistakes

1. **Using headings for styling, not structure** — making a bold sentence
   an H3 just because it looks nice, with no actual section underneath it.
2. **Multiple H1s on one page** — confuses search engines about what the
   page is actually about.
3. **Headings that don't describe their section** — "More Thoughts" or
   "Continued" tell a reader nothing about whether to keep reading.
