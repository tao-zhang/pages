Writing one post at a time, start to finish, is the slowest way to hit a
weekly cadence — every switch between researching, writing, and editing
costs you momentum. Batching fixes that by grouping the same *type* of
work together across multiple posts at once.

## Why context-switching is the real cost

Research, drafting, and editing use different mental modes. Research is
open-ended and exploratory. Drafting is generative and wants momentum.
Editing is critical and wants distance from the draft. Doing all three for
one post, then starting over for the next, means re-entering each mode
from scratch every time. Batching lets you stay in one mode across several
posts before switching.

## The three-block system

1. **Research block**: Pick every topic for the batch (a week or two of
   the [content calendar](/blog/content-calendar-template-for-small-teams))
   and gather the angle, keyword hypothesis, and any internal posts worth
   linking to for each one — before writing a single sentence.
2. **Drafting block**: Write all the drafts back to back, using the
   research block's notes. Don't edit while drafting; a rough draft you
   can fix later beats a polished paragraph that stalls momentum.
3. **Editing block**: Come back after a break — ideally the next day — and
   edit everything as a separate pass. Distance from the draft makes
   problems easier to spot.

## Why this fits a 4-posts-a-week cadence

Batching two weeks of posts (8 drafts) in one sitting per block is usually
faster than writing 8 posts individually across 8 separate sessions,
because you pay the context-switching cost once per block instead of once
per post. It also means a bad day doesn't cost you a whole week's post —
you've already got a buffer sitting in the queue.

## Where this breaks down

Batching works for pillar content — how-tos, checklists, evergreen guides
— where the angle doesn't depend on that day's events. It works less well
for anything time-sensitive or reactive, which should stay a one-off,
written close to publish.

## The checklist before you ship a batch

Run every draft through the same [on-page SEO
checklist](/blog/on-page-seo-checklist) at the end of the editing block,
not the drafting block — checking headings, links, and meta descriptions
one post at a time during drafting just reintroduces the context-switching
cost you were trying to avoid.
