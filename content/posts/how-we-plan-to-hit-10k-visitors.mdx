We started this blog with no domain history, no backlinks, and no traffic.
The goal is 10,000 monthly organic visitors by October. Here's the actual
plan, not the highlight reel.

## The cadence

Four posts a week, every week, with no gaps. Consistency is the lever we
control most directly — search engines reward freshness and depth over
time, and a stalled calendar is the easiest way to stall growth. Our full
[content calendar](/blog/content-calendar-template-for-small-teams)
approach is public because we'd rather be accountable to it.

## The content pillars

We're not writing about everything. Four buckets, each earning its slot
for a reason:

1. **SEO fundamentals** — highest search volume of anything we could write
   for a new domain. Beginner-friendly, evergreen, and doesn't depend on
   us having authority yet. See our [on-page SEO
   checklist](/blog/on-page-seo-checklist) as the first example.
2. **Content operations** — templates and systems for running a
   publishing calendar without a big team. Practical, specific, linkable.
3. **AI-assisted marketing** — how we're actually using AI in our own
   workflow, written honestly about where it helps and where it doesn't.
4. **Build in public** — this post is one. Traffic numbers, what's
   working, what isn't. Transparency is also just good content: nobody
   else can write our specific numbers.

## What's already in place

The technical side matters as much as the writing:

- Every page ships with a title tag, meta description, canonical URL, and
  Open Graph/Twitter tags.
- Structured data (`BlogPosting` JSON-LD) on every post so search engines
  can parse it correctly.
- An automatically generated sitemap and RSS feed — no manual index to
  maintain, so nothing gets missed.
- Fast, static pages with no client-side data fetching slowing down the
  content that matters for rankings.

## What we don't have yet

No paid keyword research tooling, no analytics baseline, and no backlinks
beyond whatever this content earns organically. We're treating that as
the honest starting line, not a footnote.

## How we'll know it's working

Once analytics are live, we'll report which posts and pillars are
actually driving traffic — and adjust the calendar based on what the data
says, not what we assumed going in. Underperforming topics get cut;
overperforming ones get more of the calendar.
