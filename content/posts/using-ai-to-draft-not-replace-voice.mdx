AI drafting tools are good at getting words on a page fast and mediocre at
sounding like anyone in particular. That gap is the whole story — where
you land depends entirely on how you use the tool, not whether you use it.

## Where AI drafting genuinely helps

- **Getting past the blank page.** A rough first pass, even a mediocre
  one, is faster to edit than an empty document is to fill.
- **Structural scaffolding.** Generating an outline or heading structure
  for a [checklist-style post](/blog/on-page-seo-checklist) is a
  mechanical task AI handles well, freeing you to focus on the specifics
  that make it useful.
- **Batch-friendly repetitive work.** Rewriting the same explanation for a
  different audience, or drafting several similarly-structured posts in
  one sitting, benefits from the same [batching
  approach](/blog/batch-writing-content-schedule) whether a human or a
  model is doing the drafting pass.

## Where it hurts

- **Generic phrasing that could describe anything.** Left unedited, AI
  drafts tend toward safe, hedge-everything language that says nothing
  specific — the opposite of what actually ranks or gets read.
- **Confident-sounding claims with no basis.** A draft can state something
  plausible-sounding and wrong with the same tone as something true. Every
  factual claim needs a human check before publish.
- **Losing the specific voice that makes content worth reading.** If every
  post reads like it could have been written by any company about any
  product, you've lost the differentiation that makes someone come back.

## The editing habit that matters most

Read the draft and ask: **could a generic competitor have published this
exact post?** If yes, it needs specifics only you have — actual numbers,
actual decisions, actual mistakes. That's also the litmus test we use for
[build-in-public content](/blog/how-we-plan-to-hit-10k-visitors): nobody
else can write our specific traffic numbers, so leaning into that
specificity is where AI-assisted drafting stops being generic.

## What this means in practice

AI-assisted drafting doesn't remove the editing step — it moves the effort
from generating words to shaping them. Every post here still goes through
the same [on-page SEO checklist](/blog/on-page-seo-checklist) and a
voice-and-accuracy pass regardless of how the first draft got written. The
tool changes where the time goes, not whether the editing happens.
