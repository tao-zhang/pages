The title tag is the single most important on-page element you control,
and most people either stuff it with keywords or forget it's not the same
thing as the H1. Here's how to write one that does its job.

## What a title tag actually does

It's the blue link text in search results and the tab title in a browser.
It's also one of the strongest on-page ranking signals left — it tells
both Google and the searcher what the page is about before either of them
reads a word of your content. Get it wrong and you lose clicks even when
you've earned the ranking.

## The mechanical rules

1. **Keep it under ~60 characters.** Google truncates longer titles in
   search results, usually cutting off mid-word in a way that looks sloppy.
2. **Put your primary keyword near the front.** Search engines weight
   earlier words slightly more, and readers scanning results decide in the
   first few words whether a page is relevant.
3. **Make every title on the site unique.** Duplicate titles confuse
   search engines about which page to rank for a given query and make your
   own site compete with itself.
4. **Don't confuse the title tag with the H1.** They can match, but the
   title tag is written for search results and the H1 for the reader
   already on the page — slightly different jobs, slightly different
   audiences.

## Patterns that actually work

- **The how-to**: "How to [Do Thing]: [Specific Detail]" — works because it
  matches the exact phrasing people search.
- **The number**: "[N] Things to Check Before [Outcome]" — see our [on-page
  SEO checklist](/blog/on-page-seo-checklist) for an example that leans on
  this pattern.
- **The comparison or definition**: "[Term] Explained for [Audience]" —
  works well for beginner-intent searches, like our [Core Web Vitals
  explainer](/blog/core-web-vitals-for-non-engineers).

## Patterns to avoid

- **Clickbait with no keyword.** "You Won't Believe This SEO Trick" might
  get a curious click once, but it doesn't match how anyone actually
  searches, so it won't get found in the first place.
- **Keyword stuffing.** "SEO Tips, SEO Guide, SEO Help, SEO Advice" reads
  as spam to both readers and search engines and can actively hurt
  rankings.
- **Vague internal jargon.** A title that only makes sense to your team
  doesn't make sense to a searcher who has never heard of your product.

## A quick test

Read your title tag out loud as if you were the person searching, not the
person who wrote the post. If it doesn't sound like something you'd
actually type into Google, rewrite it. This pairs with the [meta
description](/blog/how-to-write-a-meta-description) sitting right below
it — together they're your pitch for the click, before anyone even reaches
the page.
