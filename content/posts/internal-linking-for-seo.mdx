Internal linking gets less attention than keywords or backlinks, but for
a small or new site it's one of the few SEO levers entirely in your
control — no outreach, no budget, no waiting on someone else.

## Why it matters

- **It helps search engines find and understand your pages.** A post with
  no internal links pointing to it is harder to discover and looks less
  important in the site's structure.
- **It distributes authority.** A page that earns backlinks or ranks well
  can pass some of that value to related posts it links to.
- **It keeps readers on the site.** Someone reading a checklist post who
  clicks through to a related how-to is a second pageview and a lower
  bounce rate, both of which correlate with better engagement signals.

## The system

You don't need a linking strategy meeting. You need a habit, applied at
two points:

**When you publish a new post:** link out to 2–3 existing, relevant posts
from within the body — not a "related posts" widget bolted on at the
bottom, but links placed where they're actually useful to a reader mid-
sentence. This post links to our [on-page SEO
checklist](/blog/on-page-seo-checklist) and our [content calendar
template](/blog/content-calendar-template-for-small-teams) because both
are genuinely relevant here.

**When you publish a new post, go back and update 1–2 older posts** to
link forward to it, if relevant. This is the step most teams skip, and
it's the one that actually keeps older content connected to newer,
better-optimized posts instead of going stale in isolation.

## What to link, and how

- **Use descriptive anchor text**, not "click here." The anchor text
  itself is a small relevance signal — "on-page SEO checklist" tells you
  (and search engines) more than "this post."
- **Link where it's contextually earned**, not just to hit a quota. A
  forced link reads as forced.
- **Don't over-link a single post.** A handful of well-placed links beats
  a dozen scattered through every paragraph.

## A lightweight way to track it

For a small blog, a spreadsheet with one row per post and a column for
"linked from" is enough — no dedicated tooling required. When you publish
a new post, scan that sheet for 1–2 older posts to update. It takes a few
minutes and compounds every week.

## The payoff

None of this shows up immediately. Internal linking is a compounding
habit — the 40th post in a well-linked site structure has a much easier
time ranking than the 40th post published in isolation. Start the habit
on post one.
